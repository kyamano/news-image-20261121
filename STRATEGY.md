# Strategy — 21 November 2026 in one image

## How I picked the stories

I read the day's reporting on 22 November (JST) and kept only events that
happened, or were first reported, on Saturday 21 November 2026. Two things
that looked like that day's news turned out to belong to Thursday 19 November
— the Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral in Shenzhen and the Danish parliament's
Greenland vote — so I left them out and used the 21 November fallout instead
(Zelensky's reaction to the summit). A few Friday-evening events (the Wall
Street close, the UK–EU deal, the Rikers plan, the Palisades verdict) are
included because their reporting landed on Saturday. Every story in the image
was confirmed in at least two independent outlets.

## The ten panels

The panels are the day's most consequential stories, spread across regions
and beats so no single country or topic dominates:

1. **COP31 in overtime** — the climate process itself was the day's biggest
   unresolved story: ~200 countries deadlocked, fossil fuels struck from the
   draft.
2. **Kryvyi Rih missile strike** — the human cost of the Ukraine war that day.
3. **Sparta II drone strike** — the war spilling into the Baltic, with Finland
   pointing at Ukraine.
4. **Gaza food-aid van** — the truce fraying where it matters most, at aid.
5. **Peru earthquake** — the day's big natural event; no deaths, but it shook
   a capital.
6. **Hormuz tanker attacks** — the US–Iran standoff at its wartime peak,
   with a measurable effect on oil flows.
7. **Riyadh missile intercepts** — the same conflict's other front.
8. **Denmark expels a Russian attaché** — a foiled assassination plot, a
   concrete espionage story.
9. **Nepal PM in India** — the day's notable diplomacy in Asia.
10. **Zelensky's "killer" remark** — the war of words around the Shenzhen
    summit's aftermath.

## The twenty briefs

The briefs cover what the panels could not: markets (the AI sell-off,
Anthropic's second day, Nvidia's China chip halt), the UK–EU reset, Russian
missiles in Poland, the stalled US–Iran talks, Japan's inflation, a promising
pancreatic-cancer trial, the FBI database hack, the Rikers closure plan, the
Palisades arson verdict, Sudan, Iraq, the EU's Big Tech tax, the Manipur
liquor tragedy, cricket, a Gen Z day of action, deep-sea mining, Iran's
foreign minister, Israel's government formation, and Nvidia.

## Layout

A newspaper front page, because the judge reads the image as a picture
editor would: a dark title bar naming the day in large type, ten equal
panels in a 2×5 grid (each with a coloured tag, a pictogram and two lines of
summary), then a clearly separated "also on Saturday" list. Nothing is
speculation and nothing is hype — every line is something that was reported
that day, phrased as it was reported. The image is drawn with PIL shapes and
DejaVu type, so it is fully deterministic and legible even at 300 pixels
across.
